Attention is the raw material of a good life. Everything you build — skills, relationships, work — depends on your ability to focus.

Why focus is so hard now

Constant notifications fragment attention. Research by Gloria Mark at UC Irvine has documented how often knowledge workers switch between screens and tasks, and how interruptions add stress. Rebuilding focus is less about willpower and more about environment design.

Deep work, defined

Cal Newport describes deep work as professional activity performed in distraction-free concentration that pushes your abilities to their limit. Most people can sustain a few hours a day at most — which is why protecting even one or two hours matters.

The 7 rules

  1. Time-block 60–90 minute focus sessions in your calendar.
  2. Phone out of sight — in another room if possible.
  3. Batch communication into two or three windows per day.
  4. Turn off all non-human notifications.
  5. Single-tab rule during deep work.
  6. Shutdown ritual at the end of the day.
  7. Boredom practice: wait in line without your phone.

A 30-day digital reset

Remove optional apps for 30 days. At the end, reintroduce only those that serve a clear value — and set rules for how you use them. Track "phone-free first hour" and "deep-work blocks" in the habit tracker.
