Goals are good at giving direction and terrible at producing daily action. Systems are the opposite. The trick is combining them.

What makes a goal SMART

The SMART acronym traces back to a 1981 article by George T. Doran in Management Review. A SMART goal is:

  • Specific — clear about what will change.
  • Measurable — you'll know when it's done.
  • Achievable — stretching but realistic.
  • Relevant — tied to what you actually care about.
  • Time-bound — has a deadline.

"Get fit" becomes "Run 5 km in under 30 minutes by March 31."

Why SMART isn't enough

A deadline three months away doesn't tell you what to do on a rainy Tuesday. That's where systems come in. A system is the recurring process that produces results: the training plan, the budget review, the writing slot.

Goals decide the direction. Systems decide the distance.

The hybrid method

  1. Set one SMART outcome goal per quarter.
  2. Derive a lead measure — the weekly action most predictive of success (e.g. three runs per week).
  3. Design the system — when, where and how the action happens.
  4. Plan for obstacles with if-then statements.
  5. Review weekly — adjust the system, not the goal.

Common mistakes

  • Too many goals. One to three per quarter, maximum.
  • Outcome-only goals. Always pair with a behaviour you control.
  • No review. Plans decay in about two weeks without one.
