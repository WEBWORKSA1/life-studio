Financial independence (FI) means having enough invested that your portfolio can cover your living costs. It's not about being rich — it's about having choices.

The freedom number

Your freedom number is roughly annual spending × 25. That comes from the "4% guideline", rooted in research such as William Bengen's 1994 study and the 1998 Trinity study, which looked at how much could be withdrawn from a stock-and-bond portfolio historically without running out over roughly 30 years. It's a rule of thumb, not a guarantee — many planners use 3–3.5% for longer retirements.

Why savings rate is the master lever

Your savings rate works twice: it grows your investments and lowers the spending your portfolio must support. With a 5% real return and starting from zero, the approximate years to FI look like this:

10%~51
25%~32
50%~17
65%~11

A practical sequence

  1. Track spending for one month. No judgement, just data.
  2. Build a starter emergency fund of one month's expenses.
  3. Kill high-interest debt — credit card interest usually beats any investment return.
  4. Automate savings on payday.
  5. Invest in low-cost, diversified funds using tax-advantaged accounts available in your country.
  6. Grow income — skills, promotions, a side business. Cutting has a floor; earning doesn't.
