The myth: one day you'll discover your one true calling. The reality: purpose is usually built, through action, over time.

Passion is developed, not found

Research by Stanford psychologists (O'Keefe, Dweck and Walton, 2018) found that people who believe interests are "fixed" and waiting to be found tend to give up faster when an interest gets difficult. People with a "growth" view of interests explore more broadly and persist longer.

About ikigai

The popular Venn diagram — what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, what you can be paid for — is a helpful prompt, but it's a Western adaptation. In Japan, ikigai usually refers to small, everyday sources of meaning: a morning ritual, a craft, family. That's actually good news. You don't need one grand answer; you need several sources of meaning.

Five experiments to find direction

  1. Energy audit: for two weeks, note which activities leave you energised vs drained.
  2. Good-time journal: write down moments of flow — when time disappears.
  3. Curiosity list: list 20 things you'd read about for fun. Look for clusters.
  4. Contribution test: volunteer or help someone in an area you're curious about.
  5. Mini-projects: run a 30-day project in your top curiosity cluster.
