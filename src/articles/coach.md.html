A great coach can help you make progress faster than you would alone. A poor one wastes money and momentum. Here's how to tell the difference.

What coaching is (and isn't)

Coaching is a structured, forward-focused partnership that helps you clarify goals, remove obstacles and stay accountable. It is not therapy: coaches don't diagnose or treat mental-health conditions. If you're dealing with depression, anxiety, trauma or addiction, start with a licensed clinician.

Types of coaches

  • Life coach — general direction, balance, transitions.
  • Career coach — job change, interviews, promotions.
  • Executive / leadership coach — managers and founders.
  • Health & wellness coach — habits, fitness, nutrition behaviours.
  • Financial coach — budgeting and money behaviour (different from a licensed financial advisor).
  • Relationship coach — dating and communication.

What it costs

Prices vary widely by market, experience and niche. As a rough guide: group programs often run $50–150/month, 1:1 coaching $150–600/month, and executive coaching $1,000+/month. Very cheap isn't automatically bad, and very expensive isn't automatically good — look at fit and evidence.

Credentials that matter

Coaching is unregulated in most countries, so anyone can use the title. Credentials from bodies such as the International Coaching Federation (ICF — ACC, PCC, MCC levels) or the European Mentoring and Coaching Council (EMCC) indicate formal training, supervised hours and an ethics code.

12 questions to ask in a discovery call

  1. What training and credentials do you have?
  2. Who do you typically work with?
  3. What results have similar clients achieved?
  4. What's your coaching approach?
  5. How do sessions work, and how often?
  6. What happens between sessions?
  7. How do we measure progress?
  8. What's the total cost and commitment?
  9. What's your cancellation / refund policy?
  10. When would you refer me to a therapist or other professional?
  11. Can I speak with a past client?
  12. What will you need from me to succeed?

Red flags

  • Guaranteed outcomes ("6 figures in 60 days").
  • High-pressure sales or "today only" pricing.
  • Claims to treat mental illness without clinical licensing.
  • No clear agreement, confidentiality or ethics policy.
  • Upsells into ever-more-expensive "inner circles".
