You already perform dozens of automatic routines every day. Habit stacking uses them as hooks for new behaviour.

What is habit stacking?

Habit stacking is a formula popularised by James Clear in Atomic Habits, building on BJ Fogg's "anchor" concept from Tiny Habits:

After I [current habit], I will [new habit].

The existing habit acts as a reliable cue, so you don't have to rely on memory or motivation.

How to build your first stack

  1. List your anchors. Write down things you do every day without fail: waking up, brushing teeth, making coffee, sitting at your desk, eating lunch, getting into bed.
  2. Pick one new habit and shrink it to under two minutes.
  3. Match location and time. The anchor should happen where the new habit can happen.
  4. Celebrate immediately. A small moment of satisfaction helps wire the loop.
  5. Track it. Use the habit tracker; seeing a streak is motivating in itself.

25 example stacks

  • After I pour my morning coffee, I will write one line in my journal.
  • After I sit at my desk, I will write my top priority on a sticky note.
  • After I brush my teeth at night, I will lay out tomorrow's workout clothes.
  • After I close my laptop, I will take a five-minute walk.
  • After I sit down to dinner, I will share one good thing from today.
  • After I get into bed, I will read two pages.
  • After I receive my paycheck, I will move 10% to savings.
  • After I start the kettle, I will do 10 squats.
  • After I park the car, I will take three slow breaths.
  • After I finish a meeting, I will write the next action.

Why stacks fail

  • Vague anchors ("in the morning") — use a specific action instead.
  • Too big — scale down until it feels almost too easy.
  • Stacking five at once — add one new link per week.

How long until it sticks?

A 2009 study by Phillippa Lally and colleagues at UCL found that automaticity took a median of about 66 days, with a very wide range (18–254 days) depending on the person and habit. Expect months, not the mythical 21 days — and missing a single day did not meaningfully derail progress.
