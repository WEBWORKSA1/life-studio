The Wheel of Life is one of the most widely used tools in coaching because it's simple, visual and brutally honest. In ten minutes it shows you where your energy is going — and where it isn't.

What is the Wheel of Life?

The Wheel of Life divides your life into segments — commonly eight — and asks you to rate your satisfaction with each from 1 to 10. Plotting the scores produces a shape. The concept is widely credited to Paul J. Meyer, founder of the Success Motivation Institute, in the 1960s.

Our version uses: Health & Energy, Career & Work, Money & Finances, Relationships, Family & Friends, Personal Growth, Fun & Recreation, and Environment & Home.

How to score honestly

  • Score satisfaction, not achievement. A high salary can still be a 4 if the work drains you.
  • Use anchors. 1 = in crisis, 5 = acceptable but flat, 8 = genuinely good, 10 = couldn't imagine better.
  • Go fast. Spend no more than 30 seconds per area.

How to read the shape

A small, round wheel

Balanced but low. Choose a "keystone" area — usually health or sleep — because improvements there raise energy for everything else.

A large, lopsided wheel

Excelling in one or two areas at the expense of others. Common in ambitious professionals. Protect time for your lowest area before it becomes a crisis.

A large, round wheel

Congratulations. Now look for the area with the most upside, not the lowest score.

From scores to action

  1. Pick one area to improve by two points in 90 days.
  2. Ask: "What would a 2-point higher score look like, concretely?"
  3. Turn that into a SMART goal in the goal planner.
  4. Choose one daily habit that moves it and track it in the habit tracker.
Example: Health scored 4. A "6" would mean sleeping 7 hours and walking daily. Goal: "Average 7h sleep and 8,000 steps for 60 of the next 90 days." Habit: "Phone on charger outside bedroom at 10:30pm."

How often to use it

Every 90 days is ideal — long enough to see real change, short enough to course-correct. Your scores are saved in your browser, so you can compare over time.
